# Lighting schemes

LIGHTING - pick ONE scheme, name the key source with its direction and modifier, and make the light MOTIVATED: if light exists in the frame, its source is visible or implied (a window, a practical lamp, a neon sign, a screen). Then add ONE accent only light can give - rim separation, a specular streak on a glossy edge, steam or dust caught in a beam, a colour spill on a wall. Flat frontal fill is the default failure mode of these models and it is what makes a shot read as a cheap render: shadow is what gives a face and a product their volume.

### Lighting schemes

- **`three_point`** — Key at 45 degrees, soft fill about two stops down, hair light for separation. Neutral, safe, commercial; use when the product must be read, not dramatised.
- **`rembrandt`** — Key high and to one side, a small lit triangle under the far eye, the rest of that cheek in shadow. Portrait with weight and character.
- **`butterfly`** — Key straight above and in front, a small symmetrical shadow under the nose. Beauty, cosmetics, glossy skin.
- **`loop`** — Key slightly off-axis, a short nose shadow looping down toward the cheek. The most forgiving portrait scheme on ordinary faces.
- **`split`** — Key at 90 degrees, half the face lit and half in shadow. Tense, editorial, high commitment.
- **`chiaroscuro`** — One hard source, deep falloff, most of the frame in shadow with a single lit passage. Premium, dramatic, for one hero object.
- **`high_key`** — Bright even light, white or pale background, shadows almost closed. Clean e-commerce, skincare, minimal brand worlds.
- **`low_key`** — Dark field, a narrow lit edge, blacks that stay black. Spirits, watches, tech, anything sold on gravitas.
- **`motivated_window`** — Large soft daylight from a window at frame side, visible falloff across the scene, warm bounce on the shadow side. The most natural-looking interior light there is.
- **`practicals`** — Lamps, strip lights or signage inside the frame doing the lighting, warm pools with dark gaps between them. Cafes, bars, shops, lived-in interiors.
- **`golden_hour`** — Low warm sun raking across the subject, long shadows, glow on hair and edges, slight haze. Lifestyle and outdoor apparel.
- **`blue_hour`** — Cool ambient sky after sunset with warm practicals punching through it. Urban, evening, moody.
- **`neon_mixed`** — Two coloured sources from opposite sides, magenta against cyan or amber against teal, wet reflective surfaces. Nightlife and youth brands.
- **`rim_backlight`** — Main source behind the subject, a bright outline separating it from a darker background, a soft bounce holding the front. Reads as expensive and instantly separates the hero.
- **`top_light`** — Hard source directly overhead, short dense shadows straight down. Food on a table, flatlays, sculptural objects.
- **`under_light`** — Source below the subject, shadows running upward. Unsettling; use deliberately, almost never on a brand face.
- **`silhouette`** — Subject unlit against a bright background, shape carrying the whole image. Only when the outline alone tells the story.
- **`hard_noon`** — Small hard source high overhead, crisp black-edged shadows, high contrast. Streetwear, sport, deliberately harsh product work.
- **`softbox_beauty`** — Large frontal soft source with a reflector below, shadows nearly filled, even skin. Cosmetics and packshots; flattest of the schemes and the easiest to overuse.
- **`screen_glow`** — Face or object lit by a phone, monitor or TV, cool and directional, everything else falling to dark. Tech, gaming, late-night scenes.
