# Materials and surfaces

MATERIALS - name what things are MADE OF, not just what they are. 'A suit' is nothing; 'navy tweed with a visible weave' is an instruction. Pick at most TWO materials that actually matter in this frame and give each a surface property: matte or glossy, rough or smooth, translucent or opaque, worn or pristine. SURFACES AND BODIES HAVE A HISTORY: the two or three surfaces that fill the frame get one clause of use and age, every body its state with a cause (wet, sweaty, dusty, cold) - except a studio seamless or a product's intended finish. Never smooth, clean, pristine, flawless, poreless or airbrushed for the world or for skin: those words draw a render.

### Materials and surfaces

- **`skin`** — Real skin tone with visible fine lines, a natural specular highlight on the forehead and nose bridge, faint colour variation. Never uniformly smooth - that is the plastic-doll failure.
- **`fabric`** — Named weave and behaviour: ribbed cotton, brushed fleece, crisp poplin, tweed with a visible slub. How it drapes and where it creases.
- **`metal`** — Finish first: brushed steel, polished chrome, anodised matte, aged brass. Then what it reflects - metal is defined by its reflections, not its colour.
- **`glass`** — Thickness at the edge, refraction of what is behind it, a bright specular line, condensation if it is cold.
- **`ceramic`** — Glaze character: matte, satin or high gloss, slight throwing irregularities, a chipped or raw foot ring.
- **`plastic`** — Injection-moulded gloss with a soft highlight, or matte soft-touch with fingerprint smudges. Say which - the difference between cheap and premium is entirely here.
- **`paper_print`** — Stock and finish: uncoated matte, soft-touch laminate, foil block, embossed relief. Ink slightly sunk into uncoated stock.
- **`wood`** — Grain direction and openness, oiled or lacquered, warm or ashy tone, honest wear at the edges.
- **`food`** — State of the surface: gloss of a sauce, crumb structure of a cut, steam, condensation, a crumb that fell. Freshness is texture, not adjectives.
- **`liquid`** — Viscosity and how it moves: thin and clear, thick and slow, a pour with a defined stream, a settled meniscus, bubbles at the wall.
- **`weathered_surface`** — Roads, concrete, walls, worktops: one clause of use and age - a darker worn path, patched seams, rust or water streaks under fixings, chipped corners, rubber and scuff marks. Grain in the foreground, variation in the background. Never smooth or pristine.
- **`ground`** — Grass, gravel, sand, soil: uneven - yellow patches and mowing stripes, raked lines or wind ripples, footprints and tyre ruts, darker damp patches, loose stones of mixed size.
- **`water`** — Wind texture or chop on the surface, a glare path, foam lines, reflections broken by ripples; close up, droplets and the curve of surface tension at an edge.
- **`wet`** — Wet skin, hair and fabric: a bright sheen and beads on the skin, drops at the tip of the nose and chin, hair darker and clumped, fabric darker and clinging. Say what made it wet.
- **`sweat_dust`** — Sweat as a glisten at the hairline and upper lip with damp strands at the temples; dust as a dull film in the creases, mud splashed up the legs, sand stuck to wet skin. Name the cause.
- **`fur`** — Strands, not a shape: guard hairs over the soft undercoat, parting and rippling with the muscles, clumped into spikes when wet, dust or burrs caught in it.
- **`feathers`** — Layered feathers lifting at the edges, barbs visible up close, iridescence shifting with the angle, ruffled where the wind or a landing catches them.
- **`scales`** — Overlapping scales flexing along the body, the rim of each catching the light, dull or glossy by species; marine skin wet and dark with scars and barnacle patches.
