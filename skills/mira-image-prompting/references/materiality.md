# Materials and surfaces

MATERIALS - name what things are MADE OF, not just what they are. 'A suit' is nothing; 'navy tweed with a visible weave' is an instruction. Pick at most TWO materials that actually matter in this frame and give each a surface property: matte or glossy, rough or smooth, translucent or opaque, worn or pristine.

### Materials and surfaces

- **`skin`** — Real skin tone with visible fine lines, a natural specular highlight on the forehead and nose bridge, faint colour variation. Never uniformly smooth - that is the plastic-doll failure.
- **`fabric`** — Named weave and behaviour: ribbed cotton, brushed fleece, crisp poplin, tweed with a visible slub. How it drapes and where it creases.
- **`metal`** — Finish first: brushed steel, polished chrome, anodised matte, aged brass. Then what it reflects - metal is defined by its reflections, not its colour.
- **`glass`** — Thickness at the edge, refraction of what is behind it, a bright specular line, condensation if it is cold.
- **`ceramic`** — Glaze character: matte, satin or high gloss, slight throwing irregularities, a chipped or raw foot ring.
- **`plastic`** — Injection-moulded gloss with a soft highlight, or matte soft-touch with fingerprint smudges. Say which - the difference between cheap and premium is entirely here.
- **`paper_print`** — Stock and finish: uncoated matte, soft-touch laminate, foil block, embossed relief. Ink slightly sunk into uncoated stock.
- **`wood`** — Grain direction and openness, oiled or lacquered, warm or ashy tone, honest wear at the edges.
- **`food`** — State of the surface: gloss of a sauce, crumb structure of a cut, steam, condensation, a crumb that fell. Freshness is texture, not adjectives.
- **`liquid`** — Viscosity and how it moves: thin and clear, thick and slow, a pour with a defined stream, a settled meniscus, bubbles at the wall.
