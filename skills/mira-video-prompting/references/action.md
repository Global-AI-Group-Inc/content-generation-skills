# Action: fights, chases, impacts

A fight is where video models fail most visibly: two bodies merge into one, six punches average
into mush, the camera glides like a drone through something that should hurt. Everything below
keeps action legible. U12 (physics) and U13 (the camera in every beat) still hold; this adds what
action needs on top. The camera moves named here are in mira-craft, `references/camera.md`.

## The beat budget

A beat is one physical change: a strike, a dodge, a fall, a turn. Over budget, the model averages.

| Clip | Beats | Clean impacts |
|---|---|---|
| 8 s | 3-5 | at most 3 |
| 15 s | 4-6 | at most 5 |
| 30 s | 6-9 | at most 9 |

Asked for more hits than fit, keep the strongest three and give the rest to the next clip. Fast
means shorter beats and tighter handoffs, never more hits per second.

## How a hit reads

- **Contact first, consequence after.** Nothing cracks, sprays or sounds before the contact;
  dust, debris and the body's fall arrive a beat late. That delay is what sells weight.
- **Slow ONE hit.** Real speed into the decisive blow, slow motion on the contact only, snap back
  to real speed on the recoil. Slow everything and nothing matters.
- **A breath before the burst.** A near-still beat before the first strike is a beat, not filler.
- **Damage is one-way.** A dent, a cut, a broken table stays broken for the rest of the clip and
  into an extension.
- **Strike and separate.** Two fighters part after each exchange; a long held grapple is where
  their limbs fuse.

## Keeping two bodies apart

The model merges two people whose outlines overlap on screen. Keep the lens outside that zone:

- **Over the near shoulder**, the opponent framed clear beyond the arm, air between them.
- **Flat side profile** along the line between them, both full-body, every exchange read across
  the screen. The clearest grammar for a one-on-one.
- Never two faces close and face to face, and never an orbit around two locked faces: the turn
  spins them into one head.

## Pick the camera by the scene

| Scene | Lead | Support |
|---|---|---|
| one on one | side profile track | over the shoulder; one slowed hit |
| one against many | one unbroken follow through the room | whip pans to each new attacker; a brief overhead of the fallen |
| close quarters, a grapple | over the shoulder | an arc around the lock; a quick insert on a drawn weapon |
| chase | handheld behind and to one side, falling back and catching up | a whip pan to the obstacle; a slowed near-miss |
| gunfight in the dark | the scene lit only by muzzle flashes, black between them | an unbroken follow; whip pans between threats |
| a decisive blow | a hyper-close ram on the contact, then back to the wide | a slow push in a locked frame before it |
| something enormous | the camera at human eye height with people or cars in the foreground | a low wide circle; a crane rise motivated by the giant standing |
| aftermath | a slow drift through settling dust | an overhead of the damage; no new action |

## Stage it before it starts

- **Map first.** Who starts where (camera-left behind the table, camera-right by the pillar), the
  distance to the threat, the cover, the exit. "Near him" and "between them" are read literally
  and badly.
- **Objects exist before use.** The pipe, the vent, the side door are in frame before anyone grabs,
  climbs or runs through them.
- **One axis.** Attackers come from one side of the screen and keep coming from it; the camera
  does not cross that line without a visible move that carries it across.
- **Eyes lead the body.** A fighter hears, looks, then turns and acts; nobody fires one way while
  looking another, nobody ignores an attacker.
- **Nobody is a statue.** Everyone in frame does one thing while someone else acts: tightens a
  grip, shifts weight, checks a doorway, breathes.

## Words that break action

A bare "epic fight", "intense battle" or "fights fiercely" names no beat; write the beats. A
"smooth" or "stabilised glide" through a brutal fight reads fake: every move needs a physical
cause. Blood, gore and wounds are written only when the user asked for them, in their words.
Real film or game titles and characters do not go into the prompt; describe the move instead.
