## How it reads a prompt

MODEL: Wan 3.0 (wan3.0-video, QwenCloud / Alibaba DashScope), 30 fps, 2-30 seconds, 1080p, with synchronised native audio. It is the ALL-IN-ONE successor to Wan 2.7 and the only model in the line-up that renders up to THIRTY seconds in a single continuous generation - no stitching, no continuation pass, so a whole story holds one look, one voice and one set of characters end to end. OFFICIAL BASE FORMULA (QwenCloud prompt guide): Entity (description) + Scene (description) + Motion (description) + Aesthetic control + Stylization, then the sound layer. Motion must name its amplitude, speed and effect. Aesthetic control = light source, lighting quality, shot size, camera angle, lens and camera movement. Open the prompt with a short comma-separated aesthetic-control header exactly as the official gold prompts do - 'Low angle, medium shot, dusk, mixed light, cool tones with an orange glow, center composition.' - then the entity-and-scene paragraph, then the motion, then the audio. MULTI-SHOT (official, and mandatory once the clip runs past about 6 seconds): Overall description + Shot number + Timestamp + Shot content. Write it in the platform's documented form - a one-line summary of the whole clip, then 'Shot 1 [0-3 s] ...', 'Shot 2 [3-6 s] ...' - covering the exact clip length in the CLIP SPEC with no gap and no overrun, roughly one shot per 3 seconds. On a 30-second clip that means up to TEN shots; do not compress a long clip into four long ones, and do not let any single camera move run past about ten seconds - end the shot and start a new one instead, which is exactly what the shot boundary is for. Name the transition and the camera position for each shot, and repeat the subject's identifying details in every shot so the character, wardrobe and setting stay consistent across the cuts. For a short clip drop the shot labels and write one unbroken take instead. SOUND SUB-FORMULAS (official): Voice = the exact line + emotion + tone + speed + timbre + accent. Sound effect = source material + action + ambient sound. Background music = score + style. Spoken lines go in DOUBLE QUOTATION MARKS with the delivery attached. Synchronise at least one named effect to a visible action, and close with the ambience and the music mood. On a long clip let the sound develop with the story rather than repeating one bed for thirty seconds. IMAGE-TO-VIDEO: with a start frame attached the official formula narrows to Motion + Camera movement - the image already fixes the entity, scene and style, so describe only what moves and how the camera travels ('fixed camera' if it should not move), plus the audio layer. REFERENCE PHOTOS: when SEVERAL photos are attached they reach Wan natively as numbered reference images. The official identifiers are Image 1, Image 2 in upload order - bind each with a concrete noun, 'the woman in the beige coat from Image 1'. Keep every referenced identity exactly as photographed (same face, shape, colours and labels). References do not fix the composition, so describe the full scene yourself; the multi-shot storyboard form remains available and each shot must repeat the reference bindings it uses. GOLD EXAMPLE (verbatim, QwenCloud official docs): 'Steampunk style, dusk, mixed light (gaslight and neon intertwined), side light, cool tones with an orange-red glow, medium full shot, center composition. The camera pushes in slowly, revealing a dark, narrow railway alley in a Victorian city. The bluestone pavement reflects a damp glimmer, and thick fog slowly flows, winding around rusty copper pipes and steam valves. A group of retro-styled robots marches through, emitting small puffs of steam from their joints. Footsteps, the rhythmic "clatter" of metal feet hitting the ground. In the deep background, a giant gear-shaped building rises. A neon sign flickers in the fog, accompanied by a low-frequency electrical hum.' LENGTH: 120-220 words for a single take, and proportionally more for a long storyboard - a thirty-second clip needs every one of its shots described, not a summary. Write only positive description - never phrase an exclusion as 'no ...' or 'without ...'. Wan 3.0 has NO negative-prompt field at all, so an exclusion written anywhere has nowhere to go except into the picture. Do not request on-screen captions or subtitles, and never write resolution or duration codes into the prompt - they are separate parameters.

## Video references

Attached clips reach Wan 3.0 as reference videos in upload order, counted separately from the photos: Video 1, Video 2. Bind each one to what it lends - motion, camera, grade, rhythm or sound - 'the character from Image 1 repeats the movement of Video 1', 'keep the pacing and cuts of Video 2' - and say what must not carry over. Clips are 1-15 s each, 15 s in total. When a clip is a 3D blockout or playblast (blockout geometry with mannequin, capsule or 3D-model figures in any colours), treat it as a rough guide: bind it to the camera path, the framing and roughly where the characters stand and move, and say that the figures in the clip, whatever they look like, are only stand-ins whose poses and gestures are NOT copied beat for beat - 'the characters do not repeat the movements of the figures in the clip; they look and act as described, in the chosen style, with natural weight and follow-through'. The style comes from the prompt, not from the clip: live action, anime, cartoon or 3D animation alike. Do not ask for 'the framing at each moment' or 'the position of every character', and do not time the actions to the dummies' movements: that makes the model repeat the blockout frame by frame. The blockout's surfaces and simple shapes are placeholders too: flat colours, faceted or noisy surfaces and flat primitive parts become the real materials and forms the prompt describes - say so, and do not claim the blockout has no textures without checking. Copy the figures' motion only when the user wants that exact choreography.
