# Universal rules

These hold on every model. The model files add to them; they never cancel them.

## U1. Order of the sentence

Subject, then action, then camera, then lens and framing, then light. Models weight earlier words
more, so the thing that must be right goes first.

## U2. Counts

Before the beats, fix how many of each thing exists and write it as a plain statement: exactly
one protagonist, exactly one cup, exactly one phone. Anything uncounted is free to duplicate.
Crowds and set dressing stay uncounted and are named as a mass.

## U3. Carriers and states

Every object has a carrier at the start: which hand, which pocket, worn where. When it leaves,
say so, and say what the carrier is left with. An emptied hand stays empty. Without the closing
statement the model refills it on the next beat.

## U4. What is hidden already exists

A face under a mask, eyes behind glasses, a label turned away: they exist before the reveal and
do not regenerate at it. Say so when a reveal happens, or the character changes identity mid-clip.

## U5. Cause and effect

Every visible change has a cause in the same sentence. A glass tips because a hand knocked it;
something grows in frame because the camera came closer. Uncaused change renders as a glitch.

## U6. Camera

The camera moves for two reasons only: to follow a subject or to change meaning. One move per
shot, with a start point and an end point, and a side for any lateral or rotating move. Pace it
with one word from the ladder: imperceptible, slow, steady, smooth, quick, whip. Do not move
through solid mass; moving through an opening is fine. Ask for handheld as "subtle handheld
drift", never "shaky".

## U7. Words that hurt

Adverbs of softness (slowly, gently, softly, calmly, serenely, leisurely, gracefully, peacefully)
are stripped by the validator; say the speed with the ladder word instead. Layout words (overlay,
safe zone, copy space, call to action, end card, lower third, watermark) are drawn as objects; say
what the frame looks like instead ("the last two seconds hold on the product, the right third of
the frame empty"). Praise words (stunning, epic, masterpiece, 4K) steer nothing.

## U8. Text and speech

Spoken lines go in quotes, word for word, and only on models with audio. On-screen text appears
exactly as the user typed it, or not at all. Never invent brand names, prices, awards or logos;
the user's own product label and location signage are reproduced as they are.

## U9. The first two seconds

A hook is an action already happening, a face already speaking, the product already in motion,
or a problem already visible. An establishing wide shot is not a hook.

## U10. Length and structure

For five to ten seconds: hook, payoff, a still closing frame. For fifteen to thirty seconds: hook
or problem, the product enters hands, proof in inserts, the after-state on a real face, a still
closing frame. Each shot at least one second.
