# Universal rules

These hold on every model. The model files add to them; they never cancel them.

## U1. Order of the sentence

Subject, then action, then camera, then lens and framing, then light. Models weight earlier words
more, so the thing that must be right goes first.

## U2. Counts

Before the beats, fix how many of each thing exists and write it as a plain statement: exactly
one protagonist, exactly one cup, exactly one phone. Anything uncounted is free to duplicate.
Crowds and set dressing stay uncounted and are named as a mass. A crowd is varied: different
builds, ages, clothes and gestures, never a row of look-alikes. So is the built world named as a
mass - houses up a hillside, blocks of a town, stalls, parked cars: different heights, colours,
rooflines and ages, never rows of identical boxes.

## U3. Carriers and states

Every object has a carrier at the start: which hand, which pocket, worn where. When it leaves,
say so, and say what the carrier is left with. An emptied hand stays empty. Without the closing
statement the model refills it on the next beat.

## U4. What is hidden already exists

A face under a mask, eyes behind glasses, a label turned away: they exist before the reveal and
do not regenerate at it. Say so when a reveal happens, or the character changes identity mid-clip.

## U5. Cause and effect

Every visible change has a cause in the same sentence. A glass tips because a hand knocked it;
something grows in frame because the camera came closer. Uncaused change renders as a glitch.

Machines move by their mechanism, animals by a named gait, thrown things along an arc: U12 has
the rule per class of mover.

## U6. Camera

The camera moves for two reasons only: to follow a subject or to change meaning. One move per
shot, with a start point and an end point, and a side for any lateral or rotating move. Pace it
with one word from the ladder: imperceptible, slow, steady, smooth, quick, whip. Do not move
through solid mass; moving through an opening is fine. Ask for handheld as "subtle handheld
drift", never "shaky".

## U7. Words that hurt

Adverbs of softness (slowly, gently, softly, calmly, serenely, leisurely, gracefully, peacefully)
are stripped by the validator; say the speed with the ladder word instead. Layout words (overlay,
safe zone, copy space, call to action, end card, lower third, watermark) are drawn as objects; say
what the frame looks like instead ("the last two seconds hold on the product, the right third of
the frame empty"). Praise words (stunning, epic, masterpiece, 4K) steer nothing.

## U8. Text and speech

Spoken lines go in quotes, word for word, and only on models with audio. A line is only the words
said: speaking without given words ("yells into the mic", trash talk) is an action - written as a
line, the model says it aloud. On-screen text appears
exactly as the user typed it, or not at all. Never invent brand names, prices, awards or logos;
the user's own product label and location signage are reproduced as they are.

## U9. The first two seconds

A hook is an action already happening, a face already speaking, the product already in motion,
or a problem already visible. An establishing wide shot is not a hook.

## U10. Length and structure

For five to ten seconds: hook, payoff, a still closing frame. For fifteen to thirty seconds: hook
or problem, the product enters hands, proof in inserts, the after-state on a real face, a still
closing frame. Each shot at least one second.

## U11. Hands

Hands are where video models break: fingers fuse, multiply or melt into what they hold.

- Contact in motion is the whole body's action and its result: "he dribbles low, the ball
  snapping back up to his hip", never "the ball rebounds into his right palm" or where each
  finger goes. The beat stays; only the finger mechanics go.
- One clear contact between people per shot. A crowd that mobs someone is a mass ("his team
  swallows him, arms around his shoulders"), not a list of who slaps and pats what.
- Hands with nothing to do rest simply: at the sides, in pockets, on the hips or knees, or one
  simple thing in one hand. Never clasped, interlaced, steepled or at the mouth - two overlapping
  hands come back as a fan of extra fingers. Background people stay alive with the body (a
  weight shift, a turn of the head, a laugh), not with the fingers.
- Hand work is either big and slow (a close shot, a held beat) or far away (a wide shot); fast
  hand work in a medium shot is where fingers fuse.
- Never name fingers, and never put hands in a negative ("no extra fingers"): naming them draws
  the model's attention to them.

## U12. Physics: force, contact, mechanism

Physics is written as an event, never as an epithet: "realistic physics", "natural motion" say
nothing to a model. Write the sequence - approach, contact, transfer of force, recovery - and the
cause before the reaction ("the cup tips only after his elbow hits it, strikes the counter, the
coffee spreads; then she looks down").

- Every moving body gets ONE clause with its mechanism or gait and the contact: a person
  transfers weight heel-first, arms swinging opposite the legs, and builds up to a run over a
  second; an animal moves in a named gait (walk, trot, canter, gallop, wing-beat, glide), hooves
  or paws striking and throwing the ground; a vehicle leads with its nose, wheels rolling with
  the rims blurred, the nose dipping under braking, the body leaning out of a turn on its
  suspension; a robot moves one joint at a time with the mass lagging; a thrown thing flies an
  arc, bounces losing height and stops. Say which part moves and what holds the rest.
- Secondary motion in layers with a delay: hair, cloth, a strap follow the body a beat behind,
  catch air on a turn and settle under gravity; dust, spray and steam are thrown by the action
  and fall back. One wind moves hair, fabric and grass in one direction.
- Mass and friction read in time: heavy things start and stop over more frames; contact points
  compress; nothing floats, slides or passes through a solid.
- Liquids and particles end in a stated state: a pour narrows, splashes, pools and stops; nothing
  flows uphill; steam only from its source.
- The restraint is the lens's, not the actor's: a slow camera never means a slow subject.

The bank per class of mover and per material is in mira-craft, `references/motion.md`. A clause
of physics survives every length cut: the pipeline protects it the way it protects the action.
