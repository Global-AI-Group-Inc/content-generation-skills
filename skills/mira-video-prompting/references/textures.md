# Textures: writing the surfaces of a real place

Whatever the prompt does not say about a surface, the model fills with the cleanest version it
knows - and a clean, even surface is what a game engine draws, not a camera. It matters most when
the reference carries no surface at all (a clay blockout, a sketch, a start frame from a render),
but it holds for every clip of a real place. A race track written as "smooth dark asphalt, green grass"
came back as a racing video game; the same track written with a rubbered racing line, tyre marks
and patchy verges reads as broadcast footage. Texture is what makes a clip look filmed.

## The rules

- **Every large surface gets a history.** Where it is driven, walked, rained on, sun-bleached,
  patched, stained, chipped. One clause of use and age per surface says more than three adjectives.
- **Write the scale the camera sees.** A wide or aerial shot reads texture as variation across the
  surface (a darker racing line, patchy grass, faded paint); a chase or mid shot reads wear and dirt
  (chipped kerb paint, dust on the barrier); an onboard or close shot reads the grain itself
  (aggregate in the asphalt, scuffs on carbon, stitching on a glove). Put the far scale in LOCATION
  and the near scale into the shot that comes close.
- **Texture shows in the light.** Say where the sun is and what the surface does with it: a low sun
  rakes across the aggregate and puts it in relief, a glint runs along the rubbered line, heat haze
  lifts off dark asphalt, wet concrete mirrors the sky.
- **Motion leaves traces.** What the action throws off is texture too: tyre smoke and rubber dust
  at the braking zone, grit off a kerb, spray from a wet road, grass flattened by rotor wash,
  footprints in sand, dust hanging in a sunbeam.
- **Two or three surfaces, not fifteen.** Describe the ones that fill the frame - the road, the
  ground beside it, the one wall or stand behind it - and let the rest follow. A list of every
  material spends the length budget and blurs the ones that matter.
- **Never the words of a render.** Smooth, clean, pristine, perfect, flawless, sleek, glossy,
  ultra-detailed, hyperreal, 8k, PBR, CGI, render: each pulls the image toward a game engine.
  Keep "glossy" only for a finish that is really glossy (fresh paint on a car), never for the world.
- **Surface texture is not film texture.** Grain, halation and lens dirt belong to the look
  (mira-craft); they do not replace a single word about the surfaces in front of the lens.

## Surfaces

| Surface | Far (wide, aerial) | Mid (chase, walking) | Near (onboard, close) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asphalt, road | a darker rubbered line where cars run, lighter unused edges, patched rectangles, faded lane paint | black tyre marks into the braking zones, oil stains, cracks sealed with tar | coarse grey aggregate, loose grit and rubber crumbs ("marbles") off the line |
| Concrete, barriers | panels of slightly different tone, rust streaks under bolts, water stains | scuffs and tyre rubber on the lower edge, chipped corners, faded sponsor paint | pitted surface, hairline cracks, dust in the seams |
| Painted marks, kerbs | paint duller where it is driven over | chipped and rubber-blackened stripes, sun-faded red | rough raised paint, grit caught at the edge |
| Grass, verges | uneven green with yellow patches and mowing stripes, darker where it is watered | tufts, tyre ruts where someone ran wide, clumps of clippings | individual blades, dry stems, soil showing through |
| Gravel, sand, dirt | a lighter band with raked lines or wind ripples | tracks and footprints, darker damp patches | loose stones of mixed size, dust lifting in the wind |
| Water | wind texture on the surface, darker gusts, glare in a sun path | chop, foam lines, reflections broken by ripples | droplets, surface tension at an edge, bits floating |
| Buildings, facades | weathered tone, darker rain streaks from sills, different window reflections | peeling paint, grime at street level, signs with wear | brick or render texture, cracked sealant, dust on glass |
| Built-up surroundings (a town, a hillside of houses) | every building a different height, colour and roofline, set at odd angles, tanks, dishes and aerials on the roofs, haze with distance | mismatched windows and doors, an unfinished top floor, patched render beside bare brick | only the facades the camera passes: brick, render, rust streaks under rebar |
| Metal (rails, fences, vehicles) | the colour of the finish with a dull or bright sheen | scratches, dirt spray behind wheels, dull where handled | brushed grain, chipped edges, fingerprints and dust |
| Fabric (flags, banners, clothes) | how it moves in the wind, faded colours | creases, stitched seams, sagging where tied | weave, pilling, frayed edges |
| Crowds, stands | a speckle of colours that moves, gaps in the seats, flags | individual gestures, hats, phones up | faces, fabric, sweat - only when the camera comes close |
| Distant trees, hills | irregular canopy in several greens, lighter where the sun hits, haze with distance | trunks in shade, a few branches moving | leaves and bark - only in a close shot |

## Before and after

Flat, what the RaceTrack prompt said:

> smooth dark asphalt with white edge lines, red-and-white kerbs, green grass, concrete barriers

With texture, the same length:

> grey asphalt with a darker rubbered racing line and black braking marks into turn one, chipped
> red-and-white kerbs blackened by tyres, uneven sun-browned verges, concrete barriers with rubber
> scuffs along the bottom, heat haze over the straight

## Living things

People and animals fail the same way places do: skin, hair, fur and feathers left unsaid come back
even and plastic - the doll look, the waxy face, the fur that moves as one helmet. Real bodies have
texture and a STATE, and the state has a cause.

- **Skin is never even.** Visible pores and fine lines at close range, a natural shine on the
  forehead and nose, colour that varies (redder at the cheeks and knuckles, veins at the wrist).
  Never flawless, poreless, porcelain, airbrushed, waxy, doll-like, perfect skin.
- **Every state has a cause and stays.** Wet from the rain, sweat from the run, dust from the
  blast, red from the cold: name what did it, and keep it in every later shot until something
  changes it (hair stays wet until it dries or is towelled; mud does not vanish between cuts).
- **Hair and fur are strands, not a shape.** Say how they move and clump: loose strands lifting
  in the wind, wet hair dark and flattened in clumps, fur parting and rippling as the animal moves.
- **Write the scale the camera sees.** A far figure reads as a silhouette with a sheen or a dust
  cloud; a mid shot reads wet patches, flushed cheeks, fur texture; a close-up reads pores, beads
  of water, single hairs, the grain of a scale.
- **Motion leaves traces on bodies too.** Water flying off a shaking dog, sweat flicked off a
  jaw at impact, sand stuck to wet legs, breath steaming in the cold.
- **Drawn styles are different.** In anime, cartoon or 3D-animated clips the texture is the
  medium's own (clean cel shading, soft subsurface skin); these rules are for live action.

| Body, state | Far | Mid | Close |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human skin, dry | even tone with a soft sheen on the high points | colour variation, a shine on forehead and nose | pores, fine lines, peach fuzz catching the light |
| Wet (rain, sea, pool) | a bright sheen, darker clinging clothes | water running in rivulets, hair dark and clumped, fabric stuck to the body | beads on the skin, drops at the tip of the nose and chin, wet lashes |
| Sweat | a glisten on the face and neck, dark patches on the shirt | beads at the hairline and upper lip, damp strands at the temples | single drops running, salt-dried streaks |
| Dust, mud, sand | a dull film, lighter patches of dust | dust in the creases, mud splashes up the legs, sand stuck to wet skin | grains on the skin and lashes, cracked drying mud |
| Cold / heat | breath clouds, hunched shoulders | red nose and ears, chapped lips / flushed cheeks, heat haze around | goosebumps, frost on the eyebrows / a sheen of sweat |
| Hair | the shape moving with the wind and the head | strands lifting, flyaways against the light, wet or dry | single strands, split light through them |
| Fur (dogs, cats, bears) | the colour pattern and a sheen that moves | fur parting and rippling with the muscles, wet fur clumped into spikes | guard hairs over the soft undercoat, dust and burrs in it |
| Feathers | the wing's silhouette and the colour bands | layered feathers lifting in the wind, ruffling on landing | barbs, iridescence shifting with the angle |
| Scales, reptiles | a dull or glossy body, the pattern | overlapping scales flexing along the body | the rim of each scale, a shed flake |
| Marine skin (whales, dolphins) | a wet dark sheen, water pouring off | scars, barnacle patches, pale rake marks | fine wrinkles, water beading and sheeting away |

## Where it goes in the prompt

- LOCATION - the two or three big surfaces at the far scale, with the light that shows them.
- SUBJECT - skin, hair or fur with its state and its cause; the close shots add the close scale.
- Each SHOT that comes close - one near-scale texture for what fills that frame (the asphalt
  rushing under an onboard camera, the chipped kerb the car rides over).
- With a blockout, a sketch or a render as the reference, never describe its own surfaces ("grey
  clay", "flat colours"); describe the real world it stands for.
