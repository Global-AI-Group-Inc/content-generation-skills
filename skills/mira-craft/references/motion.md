# Physics, motion and materials

PHYSICS AND MOTION - how every moving thing is written.
(a) EVENT, NOT EPITHET: 'realistic physics', 'physically accurate', 'natural motion' say nothing to the model. Write the sequence instead - approach, contact, transfer of force, recovery - and the model has a path to follow.
(b) CAUSE BEFORE REACTION, in the same sentence: the cup tips only after the elbow hits it, strikes the counter, and the coffee spreads; then she hears it and looks down. A reaction with no named trigger is a glitch.
(c) EVERY MOVING BODY HAS A MECHANISM OR A GAIT: a person transfers weight heel-first with the arms swinging opposite the legs; an animal moves in a named gait; a machine moves by its wheels, joints or hull; a thrown thing flies an arc, bounces losing energy, rolls and stops. One clause per mover says WHICH part moves and what holds the rest.
(d) SECONDARY MOTION IN LAYERS WITH A DELAY: primary is the action; secondary - hair, cloth, a strap, a leash - follows the body a fraction of a second behind, catches air on a turn and settles under gravity; tertiary - dust, spray, steam, a highlight sliding on a surface - is thrown by the action and falls back. One wind moves hair, fabric and grass in one direction.
(e) MASS AND FRICTION READ IN TIME: heavy things start and stop over more frames than light ones; a body braking leans forward, a body turning leans in; contact points compress - a cushion dents, a heel presses, a tyre flattens; nothing floats, slides or passes through another solid.
(f) LIQUIDS AND PARTICLES END IN A STATE: a pour narrows as it falls, splashes on contact, pools and stops; spray is flung up and falls behind the mover; nothing flows uphill; steam and smoke rise only from their source and drift with the one wind.
(g) THE RESTRAINT IS THE LENS'S, NOT THE ACTOR'S: a slow camera never means a slow subject. People, hands, cloth and vehicles keep real-time speed and real weight; slow motion happens once, only when asked, and is named as such.

## Short form (for narrow prompt budgets)

PHYSICS, SHORT FORM: every moving body gets ONE clause - its mechanism or gait plus the contact and what follows ('the horse gallops, hooves striking and throwing wet turf'; 'the car brakes hard, the nose dips, the wheels keep rolling'; 'she turns, the silk scarf follows a beat behind and settles'). Cause before reaction. Liquids and dust end in a stated state. Never 'realistic physics' as words - write the sequence. A slow camera never slows the actor.

### How each class of mover moves

- **`human`** — Weight transfers heel-first, the hips lead, the arms swing opposite the legs; a stop takes a step and a lean back, a start takes a lean forward and one short step. Running builds over a second or two from a reaction, never full speed on frame one; only trained athletes sprint. Hair, jacket and bag follow a beat behind. Typical failure: floating feet, a walk that glides; fix it with the contact ('boots plant with weight and friction').
- **`quadruped`** — Name the gait: walk (four beats, three feet down), trot (diagonal pairs), canter (three beats, one lead), gallop (four beats then a suspension with all hooves off the ground); head and neck bob with the stride, the tail trails and lifts at speed, hooves or paws strike and throw the ground. A dog's ears and jowls flap, a big cat's shoulders roll. Known limit: a hind leg that slides - shoot from the front or three-quarter, not the profile.
- **`bird`** — Name the flight: a wing-beat cycle with the downstroke as the power stroke, a glide with wings locked and primaries spread, a hover with fast shallow beats (small birds only), a dive with wings tucked. Landing: the wings flare and cup a beat before the feet touch, the tail fans as a brake; take-off is a jump then two deep strokes. Feathers lift at the edges in wind.
- **`insect`** — Six legs move in alternating tripods (front and rear of one side with the middle of the other); wings are a blur, drawn as a translucent smear, never as slow flapping; antennae and abdomen twitch independently. Spiders move on eight legs in two sets of four; a fly lands and pivots in a single frame. Keep the camera close and the depth shallow - scale reads through the blur and the macro optics.
- **`creature`** — Invent nothing anatomical: borrow the locomotion of a real animal and say which - 'a low reptilian gait', 'moves like a great ape on its knuckles', 'swims like a whale, the spine flexing up and down, the tail driving'. A body in the air rolls around its long axis and arches; it never banks like an aircraft or glides like an airship. Mass shows in the ground: dust, a shudder, water thrown.
- **`vehicle`** — The nose leads along the direction it faces; the wheels roll (rims blur, spokes smear), the tyres flatten at the contact patch; under braking the nose dips and the rear lightens, under acceleration the rear squats; in a turn the body leans out on its suspension and the inside wheels lighten; a kerb or a bump is taken by the suspension, not the body. Tyres throw dust, spray or rubber behind. A lowrider hops on its hydraulics - the body lifts off the axles, the wheels hang, it slams down and rebounds. Never a block rocking on a spring, never sideways, never floating.
- **`robot`** — Say which joint drives each move and what the mass does: a walker plants a foot, shifts its weight over it, then swings the other leg; an arm pivots at one joint at a time and pauses a beat before reversing; hydraulics extend at one speed, servos snap; a heavy torso lags the legs and settles after a stop with a small dip. Cables, hoses and panels sway a beat behind. A drone tilts into the direction it flies and its rotors are a disc of blur.
- **`rigid_object`** — A thrown or dropped thing flies an arc under gravity, spins if it left a hand off-centre, hits, bounces with less height each time, rolls or skids to a stop and rocks once. Heavy objects start and stop over more frames and dent what they land on; light ones tumble and are pushed by air. A pushed object moves only while pushed and coasts to a halt by friction. Nothing passes through another solid.
- **`cloth`** — Weight class names the behaviour: silk and chiffon float, catch air on every turn and settle late; cotton and jersey swing and stop; denim and canvas fold stiffly and hold shape; leather creases and stays. Cloth follows the body a fraction of a second behind, pins where it is held (a belt, a hand, a strap) and hangs from there under gravity; wind moves it in one direction with the hair. A hem or a cape never leads the body.
- **`hair`** — Hair moves as strands and masses, not as a helmet: it lags the head on a turn and swings back, lifts and falls with a step, streams in wind in one direction with the clothes; wet hair clumps into ropes and sticks to skin; curls bounce, a braid swings as a rope, a ponytail whips. Fur ripples over the muscle it covers and parts in wind. A single loose strand across the face reads as real.
- **`liquid`** — Viscosity sets the speed: water pours thin and fast, syrup thick and slow, both narrow as they fall and land with a splash sized to the height. A stream stays continuous until it breaks into drops; a spill spreads out and stops; foam and bubbles rise and pop; a surface keeps ripples for a beat after contact; condensation runs in drops. Nothing flows uphill; a jet rises only where something pumps it.
- **`fire_smoke`** — Flame licks upward and leans away from wind, brighter at the base; embers rise on the heat then fall; smoke rises from its source, rolls, thins and drifts with the one wind that moves everything else; steam rises only from a hot, wet source and vanishes within a metre; dust and sand thrown by an impact hang for a beat and settle. Light from fire flickers on nearby surfaces at the flame's rhythm.
- **`impact`** — Write it as four moments in order: approach (the mover comes in with its speed), contact (where it touches what, the point that deforms - a dent, a splash, a dust ring), transfer of force (the struck thing moves after the strike; the striker slows, recoils or follows through), recovery (both settle - a bounce, a wobble, a swing that dies out). Debris leaves the point of contact in a cone and falls. Cause before reaction, always.

## Materials

MATERIALS - name what the two or three surfaces that fill the frame are MADE OF and how each behaves in light: how sharp its reflection is, what colour its reflection carries, whether light passes through it, whether it glows from inside (skin, wax, leaves). Then one clause of use and age for each - where it is walked, driven, rained on, patched, stained. Pick the materials that matter for THIS frame; a list of fifteen is noise. Never smooth, clean, pristine, flawless, poreless or airbrushed for the world or for a body: those words draw a render. Living things keep real skin, hair, fur, feathers or scales and the state the story put them in.

### Materials as the eye sees them

- **`skin`** — Real skin, never even: pores at the scale the lens sees, fine lines, a flush where blood is close, slight shine on the nose and forehead, a soft translucent glow at ears and nostrils against the light; the state the story gives it - wet, sweaty, dusty, cold - and its cause.
- **`fabric`** — Construction before shine: knit, weave, nap, a visible seam or hem; matte cotton and wool scatter light with no highlight, silk and satin carry one soft highlight that slides as it moves, denim shows its twill and fading at the knees, leather creases and shines only where it is rubbed.
- **`metal`** — Finish first - brushed steel, polished chrome, anodised matte, aged brass, cast iron - then what it reflects: chrome mirrors the scene sharply, brushed metal stretches highlights into streaks along the brush direction, brass and copper tint their reflections warm, iron is dark and dull with rust in the pits.
- **`glass`** — Refraction bends what is behind it, a soft reflection of the light source sits on the surface, colour deepens where the glass is thickest and the thin rim reads clear; frosted glass scatters light into a directionless glow; cut crystal throws small caustics on the table.
- **`ceramic`** — Glazed: a smooth skin with one soft broad highlight and crazing in old pieces; unglazed: matte, chalky, drinks light. Chips show a paler body under the glaze; a rim keeps a faint ring of wear.
- **`plastic`** — Say which: matte rubberised, glossy injection-moulded with a sharp small highlight, translucent milky, clear with scratches; mould lines, a slightly waxy translucency at edges, scuffs that turn white. Never 'plastic' as an insult for other surfaces.
- **`paper_print`** — Paper is matte and fibrous, print sits on top of it: ink is slightly glossy on a matte sheet, a fold breaks the ink, corners soften, a poster fades where the sun hits. Text is flat, stable and legible.
- **`wood`** — Grain runs in one direction and shows through the finish; raw wood is matte and drinks light, oiled wood glows, varnished wood carries a stretched highlight along the grain; wear is on the edges and where hands go - lighter, smoother, dented.
- **`food`** — Fresh food is wet where it is cut and matte where it is dry: a highlight on a tomato's skin, none on bread crust; steam only from hot food; sauce pools and holds a meniscus; crumbs and drips where something was handled. Say the temperature by its signs.
- **`liquid`** — Viscosity and how it moves: thin and clear, thick and slow, a pour with a defined stream, a settled meniscus, bubbles at the wall, condensation drops on a cold glass sliding down and leaving tracks.
- **`weathered_surface`** — A surface that has been used: sun-bleached on top, dirty at the bottom, patched, chipped at the corners, stained where water runs; paint flakes along the edges; a repair in a different shade. Far: patchy tone; mid: wear and dirt; close: the grain of the material itself.
- **`ground`** — Asphalt with a darker worn line where wheels run, patched cracks, a faded lane mark; concrete with expansion joints and a tide mark of dirt; gravel and dirt with tyre ruts and footprints; grass patchy and trodden along the path; sand rippled by wind and pocked by steps.
- **`water`** — Wind texture or chop on the surface, a glare path under the sun, foam lines, reflections broken by ripples; shallow water shows the bottom through it; wet rock and sand are darker and glossy; close up, droplets and the curve of surface tension at an edge.
- **`wet`** — Wet surfaces are darker, glossier and reflect the light source; water beads on waxed or oily surfaces and sheets on rough ones; puddles mirror the sky and lights; a wet body shows hair in ropes, cloth clinging and darkened, drops running off the chin and elbows.
- **`sweat_dust`** — Sweat beads on the forehead and upper lip, darkens the collar and back of a shirt, shines the arms; dust settles on the top of shoulders and hair, in the creases of knuckles and clothes, and is wiped into streaks; mud splashes low on the legs and dries lighter.
- **`fur`** — Strands, not a shape: guard hairs over the soft undercoat, parting and rippling with the muscles, clumped into spikes when wet, dust or burrs caught in it; lit from behind, a bright rim of individual hairs.
- **`feathers`** — Layered feathers lifting at the edges, barbs visible up close, iridescence shifting with the angle, ruffled where the wind or a landing catches them; a wet bird is sleek and dark with drops on the oiled surface.
- **`scales`** — Overlapping scales flexing along the body, the rim of each catching the light, dull or glossy by species; marine skin wet and dark with scars and barnacle patches; a snake's belly scales are wider and pale.
- **`rubber`** — Matte and slightly grainy, no sharp highlight, dust clinging to it; a tyre's tread packed with grit, a shine only where it is worn smooth; a sole flexes and squashes at the contact.
- **`carbon_composite`** — A visible weave under a clear coat: a sharp highlight from the coat, the twill pattern shifting as the angle changes; matte where the coat is stripped; chips reveal pale fibres.
- **`brass_patina`** — Warm golden highlights, never white; dull and brownish where hands never go, bright where they rub; verdigris green and tarnish concentrated in recesses, engraving and joins.
- **`chrome`** — A sharp mirror of the whole scene with the horizon line bent across it; pitting and a haze of fine scratches on old chrome; fingerprints as dull smudges.
- **`anodised`** — Matte or satin metal with a coloured dye in it: a broad soft highlight, no mirror; scratches show bright bare metal underneath; corners wear silver.
- **`concrete`** — Grey with cast marks - form-board lines, tie holes, aggregate at broken edges; stains run down from fixings; moss and black damp at the base; a polished floor keeps a broad soft reflection and a dull trail where feet pass.
- **`asphalt`** — Fine black grit with a matte sheen when dry and a mirror when wet; a worn lighter path where tyres run, tar snakes over cracks, a faded painted line, oil rainbows in a puddle.
- **`snow_ice`** — Snow is matte, blue in shadow and pink at dusk, crusted where it thawed, trodden into grey along the path, thrown up in powder by a foot or a wheel; ice is glossy with cracks and bubbles inside, wet ice mirrors the light and the boot slides on it.
