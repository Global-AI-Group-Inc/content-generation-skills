# Rhythm and sound

RHYTHM - the first 1-2 seconds decide whether the viewer stays: open in the middle of the action, never with a ramp-up. Every cut must deliver NEW information: a new framing, a new object or a new meaning - two near-identical shots in a row is a scroll-away. Build toward the CTA with an accelerating cadence (3s, 2s, 1.5s, 1s beats) or hold one confident continuous take - choose one, not both. End on a calm, static, text-friendly frame.

MICRO-STRUCTURE of a selling clip: HOOK/PROBLEM (0-2s, closest framing, highest contrast) -> TURN (2-6s, the product enters hands) -> PROOF (6-15s, inserts and macro of it working) -> PAYOFF (15-25s, the after-state on a real face) -> CTA (last 2-3s, static hero frame, negative space for text). For a 5-10s clip compress to HOOK -> PAYOFF -> static CTA frame.

### Hooks for the first 2 seconds

- **`pattern_interrupt`** — an abrupt unexpected action in the very first frame, motion already blurred, eyes finding the lens
- **`in_medias_res`** — the event already in progress - a pour mid-stream, a box half open, a hand mid-gesture
- **`face_first`** — an extreme close-up speaking straight into the lens, handheld, first word already out
- **`product_in_motion`** — the product dropping, splashing or snapping into frame in slow motion
- **`curiosity_frame`** — hands covering the object, about to reveal it - an open loop the viewer must close
- **`problem_shot`** — one second of the pain: mess, harsh top light, a tilted uneasy frame
- **`motion_into_frame`** — the subject bursts in from the edge of the frame at speed
- **`text_stage`** — a deliberately calm static frame with clean negative space in the upper third, built for a 6-10 word overlay
- **`before_state`** — the flat, dull, unretouched 'before' - low contrast, desaturated, waiting to be fixed
- **`asmr_macro`** — a macro texture in slow motion with crisp foley - fizz, crackle, spread

### Cut patterns

- **`cut_on_action`** — cut in the middle of a gesture - the hand starts lifting the cup in one shot and lands it in the next
- **`match_cut`** — two shots joined by a shared shape or motion - a round saucer becomes a round watch face
- **`match_on_graphic`** — the hero's silhouette is replaced by the product in the same position and scale
- **`jump_cut`** — same framing, time skipped - poses or outfits change instantly, background static
- **`smash_cut`** — a silent still frame slammed into a loud busy one, or the reverse - the shock join
- **`whip_pan_transition`** — exit one shot on a fast whip pan, enter the next mid-pan - energy carries across the cut
- **`crash_zoom`** — a sudden snap zoom punches into the detail - meme-native emphasis
- **`speed_ramp`** — slow motion ramping into real time (or back) around the key impact
- **`move_instead_of_cut`** — the subject or camera crosses a foreground pillar and the scene has changed - an in-camera edit
- **`j_cut`** — the next scene's sound arrives before its picture - the ear leads the eye
- **`l_cut`** — the previous scene's sound lingers over the new picture - reactions breathe
- **`on_the_beat`** — cuts land exactly on the musical beat - dance, fashion, montage energy
- **`through_the_beat`** — cuts deliberately straddle the beat - syncopated, more expensive to the ear
- **`accelerating_cadence`** — each shot shorter than the last on the way to the CTA - 3s, 2s, 1.5s, 1s

## Composition

COMPOSITION - build the frame for the ratio named in the CLIP SPEC; a vertical frame is not a cropped horizontal one. Three planes of depth: something near, the subject, something behind. Keep the subject off dead centre unless symmetry is the statement (a packshot, a corridor, a stand-off). Give a subject room in the direction it looks or moves: short-side framing (facing the near edge) builds tension, generous lead room releases it. Keep the horizon level unless a tilt is the point. Let one diagonal or leading line carry the eye to the subject. When the layout needs an empty area, describe what physically fills it (a dark floor, an unlit wall, open sky) - never name what will be put there later: a frame told to leave room 'for the caption' or 'for the end card' draws the caption. Shot sizes progress with purpose - wider to place the story, closer as the meaning tightens - and every cut changes the size or the angle enough to read as a new shot.

### Vertical (9:16, 3:4)

VERTICAL FRAME (9:16, 3:4) - think in thirds BY HEIGHT: eyes on the upper third line, face in the upper-central band. Headroom about 20% tighter than a landscape frame - loose headroom reads as distance. KEEP THE BOTTOM QUARTER AND THE RIGHT EDGE FREE of the face, the product and any lettering - on a phone that band belongs to the platform's interface; let scenery fill it. Stack vertically when two things share the frame: product in the lower third, face in the upper third. Live between medium close-up and extreme close-up; a wide shot is a 0.5-1 s breath. Default to a longer lens look (85mm feel, compressed background, shallow depth of field); go wide only when vertical architecture or a full-body move demands it. Leading lines run VERTICALLY - stairways, door frames, bottles, standing figures.

### Horizontal (16:9, 4:3)

HORIZONTAL FRAME (16:9, 4:3) - think in thirds BY WIDTH: the subject on the left or right third line, the eyes on the upper third, and the open two thirds on the side the subject looks or moves toward. Two people share the width: an over-the-shoulder pair, then the reverse single on the opposite third so the eyelines meet across the cut. Use the full range of sizes: a wide or establishing frame can hold for seconds because the width carries the place, medium shots carry dialogue and action, close-ups are earned accents. Normal headroom - a close-up trims just above the hairline, a medium shot keeps real air above the head. Lenses from wide to normal (24-50mm feel) for places and movement across the frame, longer for portraits and compression. Leading lines run along the width or into depth - a road, a table, a corridor, a shoreline - and staging in depth pays off: a soft foreground figure at one edge, the action sharp in the middle distance.

### Cinemascope (21:9)

CINEMASCOPE FRAME (21:9) - the frame is a strip: compose in width, never stack things vertically. Faces sit high, on the upper third line, and off to one side with a broad band of world beside them; a close-up trims the top of the head so the eyes stay high. Use the edges: two characters at opposite edges with the empty gap between them as the tension, a lone figure small against a vast landscape. Wide shots are the format's strength - let them hold and let movement travel across the width. The lens reads anamorphic: horizontal flares, oval bokeh, gentle falloff at the edges. A single small subject centred in empty width reads as isolation - use it only when that is the point.

### Square (1:1)

SQUARE FRAME (1:1) - the frame has no direction of its own, so the composition gives it one: a strong diagonal, a firm off-centre placement, or a deliberate centre for a calm graphic look. A face sits slightly above centre with the eyes on the upper third. Medium and close sizes carry the square; a wide shot needs one bold shape to hold it together. Keep a margin on all four sides - previews crop the edges.

## Sound

SOUND - name every layer or the model invents one: (1) an ambient bed that places the scene (room tone, street, cafe murmur) - never leave sterile silence unless silence IS the point, then say so explicitly; (2) diegetic foley for what hands and products do - clicks, pours, fabric, fizz, close-mic crispness for ASMR value; (3) one clear music intention - style, tempo, where it builds; or 'no music' said outright; (4) an audio hook in the first second when the format allows - a snap, a fizz, a first word - sound stops the scroll as hard as the image; (5) one beat of engineered silence right before the payoff hits twice as hard; (6) dialogue short, attributed, in its stated language; (7) CAMERA-EVENT FOLEY when the move is the concept - a rising whoosh synced to a dive or a whip, a hard impact when the camera is struck, the clatter of it landing, and a drop to near-silence on the settle - each sound tied to the phase of the move, not to the scene in general. The dynamic arc mirrors the picture: quiet open, build through the proof, peak on the CTA.
